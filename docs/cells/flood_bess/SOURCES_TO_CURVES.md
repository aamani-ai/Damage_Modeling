Damage-curve review · flood × battery storage

# Flood × battery curve, where its numbers come from

A flood curve for a battery storage plant answers one question: **when water stands at a given depth on the ground at a battery container or an inverter unit, what share of that unit’s value is lost?** Unlike hail and solar, where laboratory counts give dots to draw a line through, nothing here was measured, and no flood of a utility battery plant is documented anywhere.

The battery containers follow a single rule rather than a curve, a jump from nothing to everything at 0.30 m of water, approved by the owner as a conservative policy. The inverter units borrow the flood and solar inverter table, moved up to the same entry height, except in salt water, where they follow the same jump.

The dashboard calls the cell “our judgment” overall and the inverter curve “borrowed” (T3): there, borrowed means a number inferred from evidence about related equipment, here a table the flood and solar author chose for solar inverters.

## Curves 1 and 3: the 0.30 m replacement step (battery containers in any water, inverter units in salt water)

Curves 1 and 3 · nothing at or below 0.30 m, total replacement above it · our judgment (T4), an owner-approved policy

- **Curve 1:** Battery container · any water, fresh or salt
- **Curve 3:** Inverter unit · salt water (the same step, drawn as the same line)

**What each source says, and what that means for the line**

- **Owner rulings BF-Q01 and BF-Q02, 18 October** (the Damage team’s own choice)
  - water above 0.30 m: replace all active battery hardware, fresh or salt; in salt water, also replace the inverter unit (our judgment)
- **[#1 · Hazard battery reference plant r1](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Hazard_Modeling/tree/d12718f713925ebc75f6efcc82285e5bc11e0458/docs/plans/_cross_cutting/bess_conus)** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - the reference design; claim BF-C05 records its equipment mounted 0.30 m above grade, a design value, not a measured entry
- **#15 and #2 · Sungrow manual, NEMA GD 1** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - a flooded battery must not be reused; wet gear needs the maker’s evaluation, salt is worse; no number

**On the Damage dashboard:** [1 · battery container](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/flood_bess/curves/BESS_ACTIVE_CONTACT_STATE) · [3 · inverter unit, salt water](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/flood_bess/curves/BESS_PCS_SALINE_CONTACT_STATE)

This is not a measured curve but a rule. For each of the 80 battery containers in the reference plant, worth about 901,000 dollars of active hardware each (72.1 million dollars in all), the cell asks one question: is the water more than 0.30 m above the ground at the unit? If it is, every battery module, management board and cooling unit inside is counted as a total loss; if it is not, nothing is lost.

The same rule is applied to the 20 separate inverter units when the water is salt. Nothing on this curve was measured. The 0.30 m height and the replace-everything consequence are both our judgment: the owner approved them on 18 October as a conservative policy for local testing (ruling BF-Q01 for the containers, BF-Q02 for salt water), and the cell’s tier table files them as T4.

The claim register ties the 0.30 m to the reference plant’s assumed equipment mounting height (claim BF-C05, source [#1](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Hazard_Modeling/tree/d12718f713925ebc75f6efcc82285e5bc11e0458/docs/plans/_cross_cutting/bess_conus)) and notes that Hazard’s rain-flood depths stop at the same 0.30 m (source [#8](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Hazard_Modeling/blob/c6a5b331a165e1b4e4246ddf90017e2c76c598a1/perils/flood/src/flood/pluvial/ponding.py)), so rain flooding never triggers the step. No document gives the height at which water actually gets into a container. The sources support the direction of the rule, not its numbers.

Sungrow’s manual for an adjacent battery product says a flooded battery must not be reused (source [#15](https://uk.sungrowpower.com/upload/file/20250603/EN_UM_SBS050_User_Manual.pdf)); NEMA’s guide says water-damaged equipment must be evaluated by its maker and that salt water matters (source [#2](https://www.nema.org/docs/default-source/standards-document-library/nema-gd-1-2016-evaluating-water-damaged-electrical-equipment-guide.pdf)). Neither gives a depth or a share of value. There is no gradual halfway point: at 0.30 m or less the loss is zero, and at anything more the loss is the whole container.

The curve is used for every battery container in fresh or salt water, and for the inverter units in salt water. The review (v5, A.10) calls this fine as an upper bound with the label shown, but not as an average loss, because clean water in an electrical enclosure is often a cleaning and partial-replacement job; it also notes that the coastal flood data arrive in one-foot bands whose first band straddles 0.30 m.

## Curves 2a and 2b: separate inverter units in fresh water (the solar inverter table, moved up to the 0.30 m entry)

Curves 2a and 2b · inverter units, fresh water: half lost at 0.40 m of water · borrowed shape (T3) on an entry height that is our judgment (T4)

- **Curve 2a:** Inverter unit, fresh water · before: the flood and solar inverter table on its own axis (reference)
- **Curve 2b:** Inverter unit, fresh water · the same rows starting at 0.30 m above grade (the cell’s curve)

**What each source says, and what that means for the line**

- **[#6 · flood and solar inverter table](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/flood_solar/current/flood_solar__model_v1_0__docs_r4__curve_artifact.json)** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - 5 % at 2 cm, 25 % at 5 cm, 75 % at 15 cm, 95 % at 30 cm, 100 % from 60 cm of water above the cabinet base; copied unchanged
- **[#12 · SMA inverter station manual](https://files.sma.de/downloads/MVPS-S2-SCS-US-B8-SH-en-11.pdf)** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - storage inverters have several protection zones and need evaluation after a flood; no number
- **Damage team: entry at 0.30 m** (the Damage team’s own choice)
  - start the solar table only once water is 0.30 m above grade; stay at 100 % past the last row (our judgment)

**On the Damage dashboard:** [2a · the flood and solar inverter table it starts from](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/flood_solar/curves/FS_INV) · [2b · inverter unit, fresh water](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/flood_bess/curves/BESS_PCS_INUNDATION_PROXY)

The 20 separate inverter units (power conversion systems) are worth 520,000 dollars each, 10.4 million dollars in all. In fresh water they use the flood and solar cell’s inverter table exactly as it is (source [#6](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/flood_solar/current/flood_solar__model_v1_0__docs_r4__curve_artifact.json)), with one change: the table starts counting only once the water is 0.30 m above the ground, the same entry height as the containers.

Curve 2a is the solar table on its own axis; curve 2b is the same nine rows moved 0.30 m to the right. Nothing here was measured either. The solar table’s rows, 5 % at 2 cm of water above the cabinet base rising to 100 % at 60 cm, were chosen by the flood and solar author from the behaviour of wet electronics, which is why the dashboard calls them borrowed (T3) rather than published.

The 0.30 m entry and the decision to stay at 100 % past the last row are new and are our judgment (T4; claims BF-C14 and BF-C26). SMA’s manual for a storage inverter station (source [#12](https://files.sma.de/downloads/MVPS-S2-SCS-US-B8-SH-en-11.pdf)) is the reason the cell treats the solar inverter as a fair stand-in: the station has several protection zones and must be evaluated after a flood. It gives no number.

The halfway point means that if fresh water stands about 40 cm deep at an inverter unit, about half of the unit’s value is lost on this curve. At 45 cm the unit loses 75 %, which is 390,000 dollars (claim BF-C16). The curve is used for every inverter unit in fresh water. In salt water the unit follows the step above instead.

The racks, fire suppression, medium-voltage gear, civil works and fieldwork, 27.3 million dollars of the 109.8 million dollars plant, have no flood curve and are withheld, not zero.

In short: flood and battery is a policy and a borrowed table. Any water more than 0.30 m above the ground replaces the whole of a container’s active hardware, and salt water does the same to the inverter units; that rule was approved by the owner as conservative and nothing measured stands behind the 0.30 m or the 100 %. In fresh water the inverter units follow the flood and solar inverter table moved up to the same entry height. No document anywhere describes a flooded utility battery plant, so the cell rests on equipment guidance and analogy, as it says itself. In the source table, the number of sources in each group is: A (the curve itself) 1, D (shape and choices) 8, E (bottom axis) 1, F (dollars) 3, H (method) 1, I (background) 5, J (not used) 1.

**Gaps worth knowing, found while building this page.** (1) The review (v5, A.10) says the origin of the 0.30 m is not recorded in the cell. The claim register does tie it to the reference plant’s assumed mounting height and to Hazard’s 0.30 m rain-flood cap (claim BF-C05); neither is a measured height at which water enters a container. (2) The cell has no derivation dossier or workbook of its own: derivation.md, the battery evidence registers under docs/extra/discussion/bess\_damage/evidence/ and the plan’s decision log stand in. (3) The dashboard tile for the cell reads “our judgment”, but its inverter row reads “borrowed” (T3), because the tile reports the tier span of all tiered parameters and the inverter row reports only its own. Both are right for what they cover. (4) 27.3 million dollars of the 109.8 million dollars plant (racks and fire suppression, medium-voltage gear, civil works, fieldwork) has no flood curve and is withheld, not zero.
