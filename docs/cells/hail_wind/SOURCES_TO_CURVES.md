# Hail × wind farm: why there is no curve, and what the sources do

This pair has no damage curve. The Damage repository holds a hail and wind farm cell, but it is a fail-closed research scaffold: model v0.1 with no curve records, kept at a strict no-go after a second, deeper evidence search in docs r2. Unlike hail and solar, where laboratory counts of cracked panels give dots to draw a line through, nothing here links a hailstorm to the damage found on a blade and to the cost of fixing it.

This page explains what the curve would have to answer, why there is none, and lists the six documents of the dashboard’s research list, then the cell’s 28 registered sources.

## What a curve would have to answer

A hail curve for a wind farm would answer one question: **when a hailstorm of a given size passes over a turbine, what share of the value of each blade, and of the other exposed parts, is lost?** The cell fixes what that share would mean: the direct cost of repairing or replacing the damaged blade, divided by the value of that same blade before the storm (deep-curation decision, docs r2).

The blade is about 282 dollars per kW of the reference turbine (2023 dollars, NREL’s cost review, register source [#HW-S015](https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/91775.pdf)), and it is the one part the cell treats as a primary candidate. The nacelle, tower, pad transformer, collection system, substation, controls, foundation and civil works are listed too, each with no curve and a gate to pass first.

## Why there is none

To draw the curve, three links are needed and none exists in public evidence. First, from the hail field Hazard has (a radar size estimate per pixel, register sources [#HW-S001](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=hail) and [#HW-S002](https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/hail/detection/)) to what actually strikes a moving blade: the blade’s speed, angle and position change the impact energy more than the stone size does (register sources [#HW-S003](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/68309/1/Macdonald_etal_Wear_2019_Repeated_impact_of_simulated_hail_ice_on_glass_fibre_composite_materials.pdf), [#HW-S004](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/48857/21/we1854.pdf) and [#HW-S005](https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/pubs/FioreFujiwaraSelig-2015-AIAA-2015-1495.pdf)).

Second, from a laboratory or simulation result, such as cracks in a coated coupon or stress maps in a flat plate (register sources [#HW-S019](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:a2976e84-b359-4b78-a907-ca7c0abb7353) and [#HW-S021](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/88829/7/Macdonald-Stack-JBTC-2024-Generating-composite-material-maps-from-numerical-simulation-of-hailstone-impact.pdf)), to the state of a real blade on inspection: no action, coating repair, structural repair, or replacement. Third, from that inspected state to the cost of repairing that blade. Blade cost models and repair-cost studies exist (register sources [#HW-S013](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1529217) and [#HW-S014](https://doi.org/10.1002/we.2552)), but none ties a cost to hail damage found after a storm.

The docs r2 deep curation (source [#1](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/DEEP_CURATION_DECISION_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r2.md)), with two independent reviews, kept the cell at a strict no-go: making even one narrow curve would need all three mappings to be invented. A lower curve, a hard threshold or a smaller cap would not make them supported.

## What the field observations do and do not say

The one operational study in the register looked at blade erosion grades across 18 UK wind farms after about two years; neither of the two hail-prone groups showed leading-edge erosion (register source [#HW-S020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/we.2540)). The cell records that as a bounded observation, not as evidence that blades are immune: hail was counted as rough annual hail days, the turbines’ operating state was unknown, inspections were coarse, and nothing was linked to a repair or a cost.

So the right treatment is withheld, not a damage ratio of zero. Pryor and Barthelmie (register source [#HW-S006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2025.114439)) show hail as a real driver of coating wear over years, mixed with rain; that is a slow pathway, not one storm’s damage, and it cannot be turned into a per-event curve either. FM Global’s data sheet (register source [#HW-S023](https://www.fm.com/-/media/project/publicwebsites/fmglobal/documentum-new/data-sheet-individual/13-mechanical/fmds1310.pdf)) gives post-hail inspection and repair-or-replace steps, which tells the cell what to record, not how often each outcome happens.

## What the repository holds, and what would change it

The cell is a validated scaffold: a curve artifact with no curve records, a capability that withholds every output, known-answer tests that check the withholding, a workbook, and the two rounds of search logs (sources [#2](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/BOUNDED_EVIDENCE_SEARCH_LOG_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r2.md) and [#3](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/BOUNDED_EVIDENCE_SEARCH_LOG_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r1.md)) and registers.

An old array labelled as a wind-turbine hail curve turned out to be a building curve with its centimetre grid relabelled as inches; it is rejected and listed as a migration blocker (register sources [#HW-S018](https://github.com/aamani-ai/damage_modeling/blob/main/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/LEGACY_EVIDENCE_INGESTION_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r1.md) and [#HW-S024](https://github.com/aamani-ai/damage_modeling/blob/main/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/LEGACY_RUNTIME_REOPENING_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r2.md); why it was rejected: source [#4](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/cells/hail_wind/proposed/LEGACY_EVIDENCE_INGESTION_hail_wind__model_v0_1__docs_r1.md)).

The coverage plan (source [#5](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/plans/hazard_asset_coverage/DECISIONS.md), decisions D-004, D-006 and D-012) records how the pair was reopened, closed as a zero-curve placeholder and kept so after the deeper review, and the handoff tells Hazard to fail closed and not to reuse the solar, erosion or old real-estate curves (source [#6](https://github.com/aamani-ai/Damage_Modeling/blob/36fbdaaf68736a2a8f9e8ed1e536039098e086d2/docs/contracts/hazard_handoff/hail_wind_model_v0_1_boundary.md)).

The review (v5, A.20, G1) agrees that no curve can be released now, and corrects one point from its first round: the reason is a missing chain of evidence, not proof that hail does no harm; owners, manufacturers and insurers may hold the records that would close it.

The fastest honest path, in the cell’s own words, is one paired event cohort from an owner or manufacturer: the hail field, the turbine’s operating record, the exact blade and coating, inspections of affected and unaffected blades, the final disposition and the work-order cost. A structured expert elicitation is possible as an interim, but it would be a different, explicitly T4 model and has not been authorised.

In short: hail and wind farm has no curve because the chain from a hailstorm to the inspected state of a blade to the cost of fixing that blade is not documented anywhere the team could find. Lab, simulation and erosion studies explain the mechanism; one field study saw no erosion at two hail-prone sites, which is not the same as no damage. The cell withholds every number rather than writing a zero. In the source table, the number of sources in each group is: D (shape and choices) 4, H (method) 2.
