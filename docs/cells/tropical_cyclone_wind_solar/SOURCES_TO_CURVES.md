# Hurricane × solar curve, where its numbers come from

A hurricane curve for a solar plant answers one question: **when a storm’s wind loads the plant to some multiple of the pressure it was designed for, what share of the plant’s rebuild value is lost?** Unlike hail and solar, there are no laboratory counts behind the main curves. The cell splits the plant into parts, gives each part a few damage states with a cost, and places those states on a wind-pressure axis using numbers the Damage team chose.

The bottom axis is not a wind speed but a ratio: storm pressure over design pressure, so the same curve can serve a plant built for 110 mph and one built for 150 mph, provided Hazard supplies each plant’s design wind.

The dashboard calls the main curves “our judgment” (tier T4) because their centres, spreads and costs are chosen, not measured; the optional satellite route is “borrowed” (tier T3), which there means a line fitted to observed site counts and stretched to a different purpose, not something copied from another asset.

## Curves 4a and 4b: the whole plant, all parts added up by value

Curves 4a and 4b · whole plant, fixed tilt: half lost at 1.49 times design pressure, 14 % at the design wind · our judgment (T4)

- **Curve 4a:** Whole plant · fixed tilt (central)
- **Curve 4b:** Whole plant · tracker (central, on its own axis)

**What each source says, and what that means for the line**

- **#13 and #66 · NLR Q1 2025 cost benchmark, value workbook** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - value of each part per kW: modules 291, racking 110, support 190 of 878 dollars
- **Damage team, assembly rule** (the Damage team’s own choice)
  - add each part’s loss by its value; support costs follow the direct losses once; divide by 878 dollars per kW
- **Damage team, three resistance scenarios** (the Damage team’s own choice)
  - weaker, central and stronger versions of every part; alternatives, not probabilities

**On the Damage dashboard:** [the cell page (curves 4a and 4b add up the parts; they have no curve page of their own)](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/tropical_cyclone_wind_solar)

This is the plant-level number the cell reports: the loss of the whole plant as a share of the 878 dollars per kW it would cost to rebuild. It is not a new curve but curves 1 to 3 added up, each part weighted by its share of the plant’s value from the national lab cost benchmark (sources [#13](https://data.nrel.gov/submissions/304) and [#66](https://data.nlr.gov/submissions/304)); site support costs such as project management are added in proportion to the direct losses, once.

Nothing here was measured and the weighting is arithmetic, so the result inherits the tier of its parts: our judgment. At the design wind a fixed-tilt plant loses 14 % of its value, at 1.5 times the design pressure 51 %, and at twice the design pressure 80 %. The halfway point means that a storm loading the plant to 1.49 times its design pressure, roughly 1.2 times its design gust, destroys about half of its value.

The band shows the team’s weaker and stronger scenarios; the cell is explicit that they are not a probability range. The review (v5, section A.3) found the level at the design wind of the same order as the one published field study, Ceferino, Lin and Xi (2023), which the cell keeps for comparison only (source [#2](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10481168)), and found that Hazard currently passes the gust ratio without squaring it, which makes served results lower than this curve (item M13).

The plant curve is used for every solar plant on the grid where Hazard has a design wind; rain, debris, surge and tornado are outside it.

## Curve 5: optional satellite-imagery route for older non-tracking plants

Curve 5 · source-matched plants only: never reaches half; 1.8 % at 39 m/s · borrowed (T3); module-value reading our judgment (T4)

**What each source says, and what that means for the line**

- **[#19 · NREL satellite imagery dataset](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2562917)** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - ① to ⑧: share of modules visibly damaged, 34 non-tracking sites grouped by gust; ⑨ one site left out
- **[#20 · the same dataset’s data description](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2562917)** (numbers on the curve come from it)
  - y = percent of modules visibly damaged or missing
- **Damage team, fit and reading** (the Damage team’s own choice)
  - line that never goes down; damaged module = replaced (our judgment)

**On the Damage dashboard:** [5 · satellite-imagery route](https://damage-curves-wsd6lcl64q-uc.a.run.app/cells/tropical_cyclone_wind_solar/curves/TCWS_PERRY_GROUND_FIXED_VISIBLE_REPLACEMENT_PROXY_V1)

This is the only curve in the cell built from observations. NREL labelled satellite images of solar sites after hurricanes, mostly Irma and Maria, and recorded for each site the share of modules visibly damaged or missing and the strongest gust reported for the storm (sources [#19](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2562917) and [#20](https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2562917); the dataset page was re-read on 13 November).

The cell takes the 34 ground-mounted, non-tracking sites with gusts from 17.4 to 39.1 m/s and draws the closest line through them that never goes down; the eight dots are the site averages of the groups that line pools, computed by the cell’s script from the pinned file (not re-run for this page).

The one site at 48.2 m/s, with 41 % of its modules damaged, is kept as an audit point and not used, so the curve is withheld above 39.1 m/s. Two things are our judgment: reading a visibly damaged module as a full replacement of that module, and treating module value as uniform across the field (claim TCWS-C103). The dataset does not say how its gust was measured, so the cell refuses to feed it Hazard’s own wind (claim TCWS-C202).

There is no halfway point: within its range the curve never passes 2 %. In plain terms, for plants like these, storms with gusts up to about 39 m/s (87 mph) visibly damaged very few modules on average. The curve is optional and is used only when a caller asserts that the plant matches the source group and supplies a gust from the same kind of record; Hazard’s grid uses curves 1 to 4 instead.

In short: the hurricane and solar cell is a careful structure filled with chosen numbers. Every number that puts the generic curves 1 to 4 in place was chosen by the Damage team, and 82 of the cell’s 93 parameters say so. The many field reports and standards explain which parts fail and how the axis should be defined, but none gives a wind and a repair cost for the same plant. The only measured points are NREL’s satellite counts behind the optional curve 5, which Hazard does not use. In the source table, the number of sources in each group is: B (measured dots) 1, D (shape and choices) 20, E (bottom axis) 7, F (dollars) 3, G (checks) 10, H (method) 1, I (background) 25, J (not used) 4.
